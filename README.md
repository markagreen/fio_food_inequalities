# Exploring socioeconomic inequalities in healthy and sustainable diets using big data
The repo contains all of the R code for replicating the study:

**Abstract**
*Introduction:* Promoting healthier and more sustainable diets is critical to improve population and planetary health. However, limited evidence has described how these intersecting issues vary by socioeconomic inequality. Our study uses novel large-scale retailer loyalty card data, adjusted for known biases, to explore socioeconomic inequalities in health and environmental impacts of food and beverage purchases.
*Methods:* Data were household level loyalty card records from a major UK supermarket retailer between 24th July and 31st December 2022 (n = 117 710). The health-related outcome was proportion of purchases that were ‘High in Fat, Salt or Sugar’ (HFSS). We estimated three environmental impact outcomes based on all purchases: total greenhouse gas emissions, total land use footprint, and total water use. Multilevel regression with post-stratification was used to estimate each outcome measure by level of neighbourhood socioeconomic deprivation, adjusted for age, household size, distance to nearest supermarket, urban or rural location. 
*Results:* Households residing in the most deprived 10% of areas purchased more HFSS items as percentage of all food and beverages purchased (estimate = 10.3%, 95% Confidence Intervals = 10.0%-10.6%) than households in the least deprived 10% of areas (8.95%, 8.83%-9.07%). When considering environmental impact, patterns varied by outcome measure. Households in the least deprived 10% of areas had a greater adverse environmental impact overall because they purchased more food. When we considered environmental impact per weight of items, households in the most deprived 10% of areas purchased items that contributed to higher greenhouse emissions per kg but purchased items that were more sustainable in terms of land use. Soft drinks, ready meals, pasta and pastry products were identified as targeted food swaps that could reduce environmental inequalities. 
*Conclusions:* Promoting population and planetary health in an equitable manner requires a multifaceted approach. Our novel application of retailer loyalty card data shows how they can offer valuable insights for informing strategies to promote healthy, sustainable, and equitable diets.
*Keywords:* supermarket; retail; inequality; loyalty card; diet; environmental impact; food; greenhouse gas emissions; land use; water use.
